## 3. Winners and timing

3.1 The season closes at 00:00 UTC on the first day of the next month. Standings
are provisional until the integrity review (section 4) is complete.

3.2 Within 7 days of the close we contact the provisional winner(s) by the email
address on their account. The winner has 14 days to reply and complete any
identity check (section 5). If they don't, or are ineligible, the prize passes to
the next eligible player in the standings, once, and is otherwise not awarded.

3.3 We aim to confirm the result within 21 days of the close and to deliver the
prize within 30 days of confirmation.

3.4 With the winner's consent we publish their display name (never their real
name) as the season winner. Consent is not a condition of receiving the prize.

## 4. Integrity review

4.1 Every season we review rated play for collusion, chip dumping, multi-accounting,
use of real-time assistance software (solvers, bots, or anyone else making decisions
for you) and any other attempt to gain an unfair advantage. The review uses the
site's complete hand records, including folded and mucked cards, and statistical
checks on how players play against each other compared with how they play against
everyone else.

4.2 A statistical flag alone does not decide anything. A person reviews the flagged
hands before any action is taken, and we may ask the player concerned for an
explanation.

4.3 If we reasonably conclude a player broke these rules we may, at our discretion:
remove affected matches from the standings and recalculate them; disqualify the
player from the season; close their account. Our decision on the season result is
final, subject to section 9.

4.4 We may delay confirming a result while a review is open, but not beyond 60
days after the close.

## Legal position (for the owner and solicitor; not part of the terms)

Checked against the statute on legislation.gov.uk (Nov 2026):

- **Poker for a prize is "gaming" even when entry is free.** Gambling Act 2005
  s6(1): gaming means "playing a game of chance for a prize". s6(2)(a)(i) includes a
  game with both chance and skill. s6(4): a person plays for a prize "if he plays a
  game of chance and thereby acquires a chance of winning a prize ... whether or not
  he risks losing anything at the game". s6(5): a prize is "money or money's worth"
  and includes a prize provided by the organiser.
- **Providing facilities for gaming needs a licence (or an exemption).** s33: an
  offence unless the provider holds an operating licence or an exemption applies.
  The exemptions (prize gaming, private and non-commercial gaming, clubs, pubs) are
  for premises or private settings, not a public website.
- **It applies to this site.** s36(3): s33 applies to remote gambling if at least
  one piece of remote gambling equipment is in Great Britain (the server is), or if
  the facilities are used there. So excluding UK players would not on its own take
  the site out of scope while the server is in the UK.
- **The free-entry rule people know is for lotteries.** Sch 2 para 8 stops a free
  entry route counting as "payment", which keeps a prize draw from being a
  *lottery*. It does nothing for *gaming*, which needs no payment at all.
- **Gambling Commission guidance agrees.** Its guidance on poker games and prizes
  treats donated prizes as money's worth and a prize at the end of a series of poker
  games as part of the gaming.

So "no stake, therefore like any free competition" holds for prize draws and
pure skill competitions, but poker mixes skill and chance, which s6(2)(a) counts as
a game of chance, and the free-entry rule doesn't reach gaming. Option A is safe now.
Options B and C need professional advice.
